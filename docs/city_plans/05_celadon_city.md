## 1. Overview

### Narrative Role

Celadon is corruption without violence. After Vermilion's overt militarism, the player encounters a city that is beautiful, wealthy, and rotten underneath. The Game Corner is a Rocket front — and Erika knows. Everyone knows. It's an open secret sustained by willful ignorance because Rocket money keeps the city prosperous.

The player must fight through the Rocket Hideout beneath the Game Corner to obtain the Silph Scope — the item needed to complete Lavender Tower. This recontextualizes the city: its prosperity is built on the same organization that caused the suffering in Lavender.

### Tone

Luxurious, false, uncomfortable. Everything is lovely on the surface. The discomfort comes from how nice it all is and what that niceness costs.

### What the Player Should Feel

- **Arriving:** "This place is beautiful. Everyone seems happy."
- **Exploring:** "Wait — everyone just... knows about the Game Corner? And nobody does anything?"
- **After Erika:** "She said nothing of substance. Every answer was a deflection."
- **Rocket Hideout:** "This is underneath the city. Literally underneath it."
- **Rival's line:** "Surge is wrong, but at least he's honest. This place pretends."

## 3. Dialogue Direction

### 3A. City NPCs — The Velvet Surface

**Goal:** NPCs should feel prosperous, comfortable, and mildly complicit. Nobody in Celadon is suffering — and that's the problem.

**NPC categories across the city maps:**

1. **Satisfied residents** — "Celadon is the best city in Kanto. The gardens, the department store, the culture. What more could you want?" These are sincere. The city IS nice.

2. **The willfully ignorant** — "The Game Corner? Oh, everyone knows there are some... unsavory people involved. But they keep to themselves, and the money goes to good causes. The gardens don't pay for themselves." This is the city's moral position: as long as the dirt stays underground, it doesn't count.

3. **The uncomfortable** — One or two NPCs who see the problem but stay anyway. "I've thought about reporting the Game Corner. To who? The Gym Leader knows. The police know. Everyone's decided it's fine."

4. **The oblivious** — Tourists, shoppers, people who genuinely don't think about it. "Have you tried the cafe? Best coffee in Kanto!"

**Condominiums (Maps 505-509):** Wealthy residents. These should feel like the beneficiaries of Celadon's arrangement. "Property values have been stable for a decade. Whatever the Game Corner is doing, it's working for the neighborhood."

### 3B. Erika — "The Aristocrat"

Erika is grace, deflection, and complicity dressed in elegance. She talks about the beauty of Pokemon, the importance of harmony, the way Celadon's gardens reflect the balance of nature. She says nothing of substance.

**Pre-battle dialogue:**
- All surface. "Welcome to Celadon. Our city prides itself on balance — between nature and progress, between tradition and innovation. Pokemon fusion is simply another expression of that balance, when practiced with care."
- If the player pushes (based on earlier choices), she deflects with elegant euphemisms. "Every city has its arrangements. Celadon's are simply more... refined."
- She does not deny the Game Corner. She does not defend it. She simply talks around it.

**Post-battle:**
- Badge + TM. She is gracious in defeat.
- A flicker of honesty, quickly suppressed: "You remind me of Misty. She thinks honesty is the same as progress. It isn't. But I admire the impulse."
- If the player has SYNTH_MISTY_REFORMIST: "Misty told you the system needs to change. She's not wrong. But change in Celadon means admitting what we've built this on. Nobody wants to do that."

**Gym trainers (8 events across maps 414/542):** Celadon gym trainers should reflect Erika's worldview. "Erika says a good trainer harmonizes with their Pokemon. Forcing a fusion is like forcing a flower to bloom — it might work, but it won't be beautiful." Preserve mechanical tips.

### 3C. Game Corner — The Open Secret

**Game Corner (Map 405, 17 events; Map 510, 2 events):**

The Game Corner functions normally as a gameplay space. Slot machines, prize exchanges — all mechanical elements preserved.

**Dialogue rewrite direction:**
- NPCs should be a mix of genuine gamblers and suspicious characters. The Rocket presence is not hidden but not acknowledged.
- A gambler: "Best odds in Kanto. Don't ask where the prize Pokemon come from."
- A suspicious man by the poster (existing hidden switch event): "You're very curious for a tourist. I'd stay on this floor if I were you."
- The existing hidden entrance mechanic (poster/switch that opens the hideout) is preserved exactly. Only the flavor text around it changes.

### 3D. Rocket Hideout — Silph Scope Acquisition

**Hideout (Maps 45, 449, 455-457 — ~48 events total):**

The Rocket Hideout is a dungeon with trainers, items, and a boss fight. All mechanical elements (warp tiles, item pickups, trainer battles, elevator puzzle) are preserved.

**Dialogue rewrite direction:**

- **Rocket grunts:** Rework from cartoon villainy to Synthesis tone. These are people working for an organization with an argument. "The League taxes fusion licenses and pockets the difference. We make fusion available. Call us criminals if you want."
- **Scientists/researchers:** Silph defectors working on unauthorized fusion research. "The League says only licensed trainers can fuse. We say the technology belongs to everyone."
- **The boss fight (Giovanni or admin):** Preserve mechanically. Rewrite dialogue to fit the Synthesis narrative — this is the player's first direct confrontation with Rocket leadership (if not counting Nugget Bridge). The admin doesn't monologue — they state their position and fight.
- **Silph Scope acquisition:** Preserve item placement and delivery. The Scope is the reward for clearing the hideout. Narratively frame it: "This device was developed by Silph Co. to identify spectral Pokemon phenomena. The League classified it. We found it useful."

### 3E. Department Store — Mechanical Preservation

**Dept. Store (Maps 292-298, 432-436, 514 — ~55 events total):**

The Department Store is a critical shopping hub. **All mechanical elements must be preserved:**
- All shop inventories and pricing
- TM counters
- Item purchases
- Vending machines
- Rooftop events
- Any NPC who gives items or teaches moves

**Dialogue changes:** Light only. Shopkeepers can have one-line Synthesis flavor ("Business has been good. Celadon's economy is... resilient.") but the primary function is commerce and it must not be disrupted.

### 3F. Rival Encounter

The rival's Celadon reaction is disgust at the hypocrisy — a key character beat.

**The line:** "Surge is wrong, but at least he's honest about what he is. This place pretends."

**Context:** The rival has seen Surge's militarism and disagreed but respected the directness. Celadon's corruption-through-politeness is worse in their eyes. This is the rival developing a moral framework that will matter at the fork.

**Additional rival beats:**
- On Erika: "She knows about the Game Corner. Of course she does. She decided it was worth it."
- On the Hideout: "They're right under the city. Everyone just... walks over them."
- The rival does NOT help in the hideout. This is the player's solo dungeon.

### 3G. Cafe and Sewers

**Cafe (Maps 406-407, 768 — 20 events):** The cafe is a social hub. NPCs gossip. One NPC can provide a hint about the Game Corner's back entrance. "My friend works at the Game Corner. Says there's a whole other floor underneath. Probably just storage." Preserve any mechanical events.

**Sewers (Maps 72, 431, 446 — 32 events):** If the sewers connect to the Rocket Hideout, their content should reflect Rocket infrastructure. If they're a standalone dungeon, treat as a side area with light Synthesis flavor. Evaluate connection to main narrative.

## 4. Player Choices & Switches

### Switches SET in This Area

None. Celadon does not set any new Synthesis switches. The player observes, reacts internally, but is not asked a direct positional question.

### Narrative Choice Logic

Celadon's "choice" is experiential, not mechanical. The player sees corruption, fights through the hideout, gets the Silph Scope, and leaves. What they think about it colors their approach to Lavender (next) and Saffron (later). The rival articulates what the player may be feeling.

## 7. Key Narrative Connections

### Backward (reads from earlier)
- Misty's reform stance: Erika acknowledges it dismissively
- Nugget Bridge: if the player joined, Rocket grunts in the hideout may react slightly differently (flavor only, no mechanical impact here — the real payoff is Ariana at Silph)

### Forward (sets up later)
- **Silph Scope → Lavender Tower:** The Scope is the gate item for completing Pokemon Tower. Without it, the player can visit Lavender but can't finish the dungeon. This creates the emotional checkpoint described in the Lavender plan.
- **Rocket Hideout → Silph Co.:** The hideout establishes Rocket's infrastructure and ideology. Grunts here foreshadow the Silph Co. occupation.
- **Erika's complicity → Saffron contrast:** Celadon tolerates Rocket. Saffron is occupied by them. The player sees the spectrum from accommodation to conquest.
